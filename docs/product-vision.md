# Product Vision

## The product in one sentence

Left You One is a small gift for moments that do not need a big one: somebody discovers a tiny digital object, realizes who it belongs with, and leaves it for that person.

Working brand and domain: `leftyou.one`.

## Why it exists

Most gifting begins with a person and becomes a search:

> I care about Anna, so I need to find her a gift.

Left You One reverses that sequence:

> I found this little thing, and it immediately made me think of Anna.

The gift creates the thought of a person. The sender turns that thought into a small gesture: “I saw this and thought of you.” This should be more meaningful and memorable than sending a message or GIF, while remaining much lighter than choosing and buying a physical present.

The product is not primarily a text generator, a source of motivational advice, a greeting-card configurator, a digital collectible, or a marketplace. Its purpose is a moment of human connection.

## The central product moment

The most important moment is the sender's unprompted association:

> Oh, this is absolutely for Anna.

Everything before that moment should build curiosity and make the discovered gift specific enough to evoke a real person. Everything after it should help the sender act on the feeling without turning it into a checkout funnel or administrative form.

The core promise is:

> You’ll know who it’s for when you see it.

If people enjoy a reveal but nobody comes to mind, the central product mechanism has not worked.

## The sender's emotional journey

1. **Curiosity** — “What am I going to get?”
2. **Discovery** — “Oh, that’s nice, funny, or strangely specific.”
3. **Association** — “This immediately makes me think of Maria.”
4. **Affection and intention** — “I want her to have this.”
5. **Anticipation** — “I think she’ll enjoy opening it.”
6. **Future curiosity** — “I wonder where this thing will eventually go.”

The sender should not feel as though they are shopping, purchasing words, configuring an e-card, filling out a SaaS form, or gambling for a rare result.

## The recipient's emotional journey

Receiving should feel like a tiny event:

1. **Curiosity** — “Why did Dimitar send me this?”
2. **Personal recognition** — “Dimitar found something and thought of me.”
3. **Reveal** — the gift is deliberately opened rather than merely displayed.
4. **A small feeling** — a smile, surprise, relief, recognition, or quiet laugh.
5. **Possession** — “It’s with me now.”
6. **Optional future connection** — “Maybe someday this will make me think of somebody too.”

The most important recipient moment is not simply reading the gift. It is recognizing that another person encountered it and specifically chose them, followed by a reveal that feels worthy of that intention.

Receiving must not resemble an advertisement, marketing landing page, social post, generated quote, or greeting-card template.

## The gift

A gift is a small, authored internet object rather than a generic sentence. Its working structure may include:

- a main thought or offered thing;
- a small piece of context that makes it particular;
- an optional closing or ritual that gives it a life with its holder.

For example:

> A little courage before your doubts wake up.
>
> For the thing you’ve been putting off.
>
> Keep it until you begin.

Gifts can be warm, funny, odd, comforting, playful, or precisely observant. Their content and visual treatment work together. The public object is not called an “One”; “one” appears only as natural language, such as “Maya left you one” or “Pass this one on.” Internally, `Gift` is the expected domain term.

## A gift can have a life

The gift is not necessarily finished when the recipient opens it. Its current holder may keep it for minutes, months, or forever. If it later makes them think of somebody else, they can pass the same gift onward.

There is one logical holder at a time, and the gift's history stays attached:

> Started by Dimitar
>
> Anna — Sofia
>
> Alex — Berlin
>
> Maya — Copenhagen

The journey adds provenance, curiosity, a reason to return, and the possibility of an unexpectedly large story. It is not a chain game. Keeping a gift is not failure, and passing is never an obligation.

The honest promise is:

> It might stay with one person. It might cross the world.

## The value hypothesis

The working future price is approximately $2, but pricing is not decided. People would not be paying for text; they would be paying to create a small moment for somebody they care about.

The important test is:

> Once Maria comes to mind, does making this real for her feel easily worth $2?

That is why a price or commitment moment belongs after discovery and association, but before the gift becomes permanently sendable. The prototype simulates this moment and does not process money.

## A possible broader future

A company or creator may eventually originate many gifts for a community:

> Linear left 500 for builders.

People could claim, keep, send, and pass them while each journey quietly retains its origin. The value to a company would be participation, provenance, and organic propagation rather than another item in a feed:

> Give people something to carry, not something to scroll past.

This possibility informs the long-term vision but is outside the initial consumer prototype.

## Product principles

- Connection is the outcome; the gift is the catalyst.
- Discovery comes before recipient selection.
- Specificity beats generic encouragement.
- One primary thought and one primary action at a time.
- Intimacy beats reach, urgency, and engagement mechanics.
- The recipient experience deserves as much care as the sender experience.
- A journey should deepen meaning without creating obligation.
- The product should be easy to experience, show, rewrite, and discard while its premise is still being tested.

## What remains deliberately open

Final pricing, free and paid boundaries, categories, inventory, rarity, exact gift anatomy, exact copy, accounts, profiles, physical extensions, company economics, and journey visualization are not settled. The prototype exists to supply evidence before these decisions are made.
